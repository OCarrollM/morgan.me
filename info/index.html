The Three Paths Forward
Path A

Military / Test Pilot Route

Join the RAF (or similar), fly jets, work towards experimental test pilot school (e.g. ETPS at Boscombe Down). This is Tim Peake's route — he had 3,000+ hours and test pilot credentials when ESA selected him.

  • + Strongest single credential for astronaut selection
  • + Builds leadership, composure under pressure, physical fitness
  • + 1,000+ jet hours can substitute for STEM experience requirement
  • − Multi-year commitment (10-15+ years typical)
  • − Competitive entry to fast-jet pipeline
  • − Less flexibility if astronaut path doesn't work out
Path B

Space Industry + PhD Route

Enter the space sector (ESA, UK Space Agency, Airbus Defence & Space, etc.) while pursuing or after completing a PhD in a space-relevant field. Build 8+ years of progressively senior technical work.

  • + Directly builds space domain expertise and network
  • + PhD strengthens application (38% of NASA non-pilot selectees have PhDs)
  • + Flexible — career viable regardless of astronaut outcome
  • + Can supplement with private pilot licence and flight hours
  • − PhD adds 3-4 years before accumulating professional experience
  • − Need to proactively seek extreme-environment and leadership experiences
Path C

Hybrid: Industry → Reserve / Commercial

Work in the space/cyber sector, build credentials, and target the ESA reserve astronaut pool or emerging commercial astronaut programmes (Axiom Space, national programmes via Axiom, etc.).

  • + Commercial pathways are expanding rapidly
  • + ESA reserve pool is a new, lower-barrier entry point
  • + UK has already agreed to buy an Axiom flight slot
  • + Cyber security for space is a growing niche
  • − Commercial seats often require government sponsorship or funding
  • − Less established pathway — rules are still being written

Phase 1 — Complete & Position
2026 (NOW)
CRITICAL

Finish the MSc Strong

Your CubeSat thermal management dissertation is directly space-relevant. Aim for the highest possible grade — distinction matters for ESA/NASA applications. If publishable, pursue a conference paper or journal submission. A published first-author paper is a genuine differentiator.

HIGH PRIORITY

Start a Private Pilot Licence (PPL)

ESA requires a Class 2 aviation medical certificate with the application. Starting your PPL now accomplishes two things: it gets you the medical, and it begins accumulating flight experience. This is relevant for all three paths. In the UK, a PPL costs roughly £8,000-12,000. Even if you go the military route later, civilian hours count. Budget for this as a long-term investment.

HIGH PRIORITY

Begin Learning Russian

Both NASA and ESA require or strongly prefer Russian language skills. It's spoken alongside English on the ISS. ESA's basic training includes intensive Russian language instruction. Starting early gives you a massive advantage — NASA candidates who can't reach intermediate-low fluency during training are disqualified. Consistency matters more than intensity: 20-30 min daily via apps + a weekly tutor.

MEDIUM

Get SCUBA Certified

Spacewalk training uses neutral buoyancy (underwater simulation). PADI Open Water and Advanced Open Water certifications demonstrate comfort in the underwater environment. NASA's astronaut training requires SCUBA certification and a 75m swim in flight suit and shoes. This also opens the door to later applying for analogue missions like NEEMO.

MEDIUM

Physical Fitness Baseline

Start a structured fitness regime if you haven't already. Astronaut physicals require excellent cardiovascular health, blood pressure ≤140/90, good vision (correctable to 20/20 is fine), and overall robust health. Focus on: cardiovascular endurance (running, swimming), strength training, and flexibility. This is a lifelong commitment — start the habit now.
